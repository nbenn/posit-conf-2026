# Rehearsal plan

Five days from Thursday 10 to the talk on Tuesday 15 at 13:05. Thirty to
forty minutes a day, in two or three short sessions rather than one long
one. Sleep between sessions does part of the work.

## What is being memorised

Every sentence, as written. The talk is 626 words in a hard five minutes,
and at the marked pace it runs 4:43, so the slack is under twenty seconds.
Improvised wording is what eats slack: a bridge made up on stage is always
longer than the sentence it replaces. Fixed words are the timing.

Fixing the words does not mean learning them as a string. The words hold
because the structure under them holds: each slide is a run of beats, each
beat does one thing for the argument, and each sentence inside a beat is
forced by the one before it. The beats are learned first, in your own words
if need be, and the exact wording is fixed on top of them against the
letters and the checks. That is how actors get a script word-perfect, and
it is the opposite of the fade-out sheets, which drilled words without the
structure.

A dot on the sheets, and `!` in `beats.md`, marks a sentence where a slip
would show: it echoes the slide, or it cues a click. Those are the ones to
check first when time is short, not the only ones to get right.

## The ladder

Eight views, one tab each. The unit is the slide throughout.

- **Full.** Read aloud with the deck, twice. Then close it and tell the
  slide in your own words, to check that you hold the argument and not
  only the text. This is the one place your own words are the point.
- **Beats.** One line per beat saying what it does. Say the beat from that
  line, then tap to check. This is where the sequence gets learned, so do
  not hurry past it. A purpose line that does not ring true gets rewritten
  in `beats.md` and regenerated: the line has to be yours.
- **Letters.** One line per clause, every word as its first letter and the
  keyword whole. Recite as written; the letters are the running check, and
  a line you cannot read off is a line to tap and read again. From here
  up, a word not as written is a miss.
- **Clauses.** The same lines, keyword only. Recite from the keywords.
- **Sentences.** One keyword per sentence. Recite from those.
- **Joins.** The deck. The end of a sentence in front, the next sentence
  behind, shuffled. Say the next sentence, show, then Next or Missed; a
  missed card goes to the back and comes round again. Filter to one slide
  while that slide is still new. The transition is what actually fails on
  stage, so this rung gets the most repetitions.
- **Starts.** Draw a slide, a beat or a sentence and go on from there to
  the end of the slide. This checks that each cue reaches its content
  directly, without a run-up from the top.
- **Nothing.** The slide picture, the clicker in hand. Tap the picture to
  check. The pocket check at the end is the whole talk as first letters,
  one sentence per line, for the day itself.

A rung is passed when you get through it twice in a row without a miss.
On a miss, drop one rung, not to the bottom.

A miss is anything that would show on stage: a beat skipped or out of
order, a sentence not as written, or a stall longer than a breath. On the
Beats rung a beat said in your own words is not a miss, since that rung is
about the sequence; from Letters upward it is. Pausing to think is not a
miss while you are still on the sheets. Once you are running from nothing,
a pause you would not choose to make on stage counts, because by then the
words should arrive on their own and the pauses should be yours.

When a word will not come, go to the next sentence's keyword and say that
sentence. Never bridge with words of your own: the bridge is where the
time goes, and a clean jump is invisible from the floor.

## Day by day

### Thu 10 · restructure

- Beats, the whole talk, once: for each purpose line, ask what it does for
  the next one. Rewrite any that do not ring true, then regenerate.
- Backwards, so every later run moves into material you know best: slides
  7, 6 and 5 from Letters through Clauses to Sentences.
- Evening: Joins filtered to slide 5, then 6, then 7, until no misses.

### Fri 11 · the front half

- Morning: retest 5 to 7 from Starts, five sentence draws each.
- Slides 4, 3, 2 and 1 from Letters through Clauses to Sentences. Slide 2
  is the long one; give it two separate sessions and lean on its six beats.
- Joins, all cards, once through, then the missed pile until it is empty.
- Evening: first full run from the Sentences sheet, standing, with the
  clicker, timed. Write down the time and the misses per slide.

### Sat 12 · joins and starts

- Morning: Joins, all cards, twice through without a miss.
- Starts: ten sentence draws, five beat draws.
- Two runs from nothing, standing, clicker, timed. Vary the room. Twice per
  run, stop mid-slide on purpose, breathe, and jump to the next sentence.
  The recovery is a skill; drill it.
- Check the timer at the click into the real app slide. Target 2:00.
  Practise the short version of that slide once, so the cut is rehearsed
  and not improvised.

### Sun 13 · variance

- Two or three runs from nothing. One too fast, one whispered, one at
  pace. Runs at pace should land within ten seconds of each other.
- Write the whole talk out from memory, once. Mark every word that differs
  from the sheet; those sentences go back to Letters.
- Hold the speaker cards for one run so the fallback is familiar.

### Mon 14 · taper

- Two runs at most, one in the morning and one late afternoon. Stop by
  the evening. More runs now add nerves, not accuracy.
- Joins once, the missed pile only.
- Confirm the venue setup: own laptop or shared machine, presenter view
  or not, clicker, timer. Print the pocket check and fold it into a pocket.

### Tue 15 · talk day

- One run in the morning, from nothing, at pace. Then no more.
- Before going up: the pocket check once, silently. First sentence in
  your head. Click.

## Where a slip would show

The dotted sentences, and why:

- The transition cue into each slide, the last sentence before a click.
- The takeaway echo on each slide, since the audience reads it while you
  say it: limit the shape and define the interface; inputs, parameters and
  one output; the block built for this use case; look out for what you
  don't have to decide.
- The contract line on the block slide, and the brick, stud, model mapping.
- The hand-off from specialty pieces into the code.
- The closing invitation.

## If the timer says so

The cuts are decided now, not on stage. In this order: the layout sentence
on the real app slide, the rollout sentence on the same slide, the thanks
line at the close. Each is a whole sentence, so the sentences around it
still join as written.
